1. Laws of Chemical Combinations
  • 1. Conservation of Mass (Lavoisier):
    Total mass of reactants = Total mass of products. (Except nuclear reactions: $E=mc^2$)
  • 2. Definite Proportions (Proust):
    A chemical compound always contains elements in fixed ratio by mass, regardless of source. (e.g., $H_2O \rightarrow H:O = 1:8$).
  • 3. Multiple Proportions (Dalton):
    When 2 elements form >1 compound, masses of one combining with fixed mass of other are in simple whole number ratio.
    e.g., $CO$ vs $CO_2$ → Oxygen ratio = $16 : 32 = 1 : 2$
  • 4. Gay-Lussac's Law of Gaseous Volumes:
    Gases react in simple whole-number volume ratios at constant $T$ & $P$.
  • 5. Avogadro's Law:
    Equal volume of all gases under same $T, P$ contain equal molecules → $V \propto n$.
2. Atomic Mass & V.D. Relations
• 1 unified mass (1 amu / 1 u):
$= \frac{1}{12} \times \text{mass of one } ^{12}C \text{ atom} = \mathbf{1.66 \times 10^{-24}\ g} = \frac{1}{N_A}\ g$
• Average Atomic Mass ($A_{avg}$):
$A_{avg} = \frac{\sum (A_i \times \% \text{abundance})}{100} = \frac{A_1 x_1 + A_2 x_2}{x_1 + x_2}$
☆ Vapor Density (V.D.) Formula:
$\text{Molar Mass} = 2 \times \text{Vapor Density}$
$\text{V.D.} = \frac{\text{Density of gas}}{\text{Density of } H_2} \text{ (at same } T, P \text{)}$
Significant Figures (Rules)
  • • All non-zero digits are sig figs (285 cm → 3).
  • • Zeroes between non-zeros are sig (2.005 → 4).
  • • Leading zeroes are NEVER sig (0.0025 → 2).
  • • Trailing zeroes after decimal are sig (0.200 → 3).
  • • Exact counts have infinite sig figs (20 apples).
3. The Master Mole Concept Map ($n$)
1 MOLE (n)
$= 6.022 \times 10^{23} \text{ entities } (N_A)$
MASS (W)
÷ Molar Mass ↓
↑ × Molar Mass
PARTICLES (N)
÷ $N_A$ ↓
↑ × $N_A$
VOL (STP)
÷ 22.4 L ↓
↑ × 22.4 L
$$n = \frac{W\text{ (g)}}{M} = \frac{N}{N_A} = \frac{V_{\text{STP}}\text{ (L)}}{22.4\text{ L}} = \frac{P \cdot V}{R \cdot T}$$
*At standard bar (1 bar, 273 K): $V_{m} = 22.7\text{ L}$. For Old STP (1 atm, 273 K): $22.4\text{ L}$.
• Empirical vs Molecular Formula Key Steps
Molecular Formula = $n \times$ Empirical Formula $n = \frac{\text{Molar Mass}}{\text{E.F. Mass}}$
Steps: $\% \text{ element} \xrightarrow{\div At. Mass} \text{Moles} \xrightarrow{\div \text{smallest}} \text{Simple Ratio} \xrightarrow{\times \text{integer}} \text{E.F.}$
• % Composition of Element:
$\% = \frac{\text{No. of atoms} \times \text{Atomic Mass}}{\text{Molar Mass of Compound}} \times 100$
4. Stoichiometry & Limiting Reagent (L.R.)
For reaction: $aA + bB \longrightarrow cC + dD$
$$\frac{n_A}{a} = \frac{n_B}{b} = \frac{n_C}{c} = \frac{n_D}{d}$$
☆ Golden Trick to find Limiting Reagent (L.R.):

Calculate: $\frac{\text{Given Moles}}{\text{Stoichiometric Coefficient}}$ for all reactants.
→ The one with the LOWEST value is the Limiting Reagent!
→ All products are formed based on L.R. ONLY.

% Yield
$= \frac{\text{Actual Yield}}{\text{Theoretical Yield}} \times 100$
% Purity
$= \frac{\text{Mass of pure sub.}}{\text{Mass of sample}} \times 100$
5. Concentration Terms of Solutions
Temp Dependent ($V \propto T$):
M, N, %w/v, %v/v Temp Independent (Mass):
m, Mole fraction ($\chi$), %w/w
1. Molarity ($M$) [mol/L]: $n_{solute}/V_{soln}(L)$
$M = \frac{W_B \times 1000}{M_B \times V\text{ (mL)}} = \frac{x \times d \times 10}{M_B}$
where $x = \%w/w$, $d = \text{density (g/mL)}$, $M_B = \text{molar mass}$
2. Molality ($m$) [mol/kg]: ☆ Best for temp changes
$m = \frac{W_B \times 1000}{M_B \times W_A\text{ (in grams of SOLVENT)}}$
3. Mole Fraction ($\chi$):
$\chi_A = \frac{n_A}{n_A + n_B}, \quad \chi_B = \frac{n_B}{n_A + n_B} \quad \implies \mathbf{\chi_A + \chi_B = 1}$
4. Normality ($N$) [eq/L]: $N = M \times n\text{-factor}$
• Acid: Basicity (replaceable $H^+$). $H_3PO_4=3, H_3PO_3=2, H_3PO_2=1$
• Base: Acidity (replaceable $OH^-$). $NaOH=1, Ca(OH)_2=2$
• Redox: Total change in oxidation number per molecule.
• Dilution & Mixing Formulas:
Dilution: $M_1V_1 = M_2V_2$ Mixture: $M_{res} = \frac{M_1V_1 + M_2V_2}{V_1 + V_2}$
⚠ HIGH-YIELD EXAM TRAPS & SHORTCUTS:
  • • 1 mole of atom vs 1 mole of molecule:
    1 mol $O_2$ has $2 \times N_A$ oxygen atoms, but $1 \times N_A$ molecules!
  • • Relation between Molality ($m$) & Mole Fraction ($\chi_B$):
    $$m = \frac{\chi_B \times 1000}{(1 - \chi_B) \times M_A}$$
  • • Density of water: $d = 1\text{ g/mL}$.
    Molarity of pure $H_2O = \frac{1000}{18} = \mathbf{55.55\ M}$ (also molality = $55.55\ m$).
